### Series B — armed (1 run, parked; capture obtained)

Run b1 parked on the first armed run, so the
series stopped with the capture in hand. Its own
summary and logs carry the same FD-77 signature
(192 MB last milestone, no window in 900 s, 0
BW64-FAIL, no fetch-layer failure lines), and its
console carries fifteen armed thread dumps
(63.6 s → 903.6 s, every 60 s; every dump
dumped=71, maxTid=165, skipped-lock=0 — the
FD-76-fixed dump enumerating the real threads of
a 32-bit session through the public route).

The dumps answer the question FD-77's entry
specifies for exactly this capture — whether a
session that parks on the fault-path-fixed build
freezes as one stranded fetcher or as the wider
constellation seen before the amplifier fix:

- The running-thread count was 10 in the first
  dump (63.6 s, startup still in progress) and
  13 from the second dump (123.6 s) on; **dumps
  2 through 15 are byte-identical per thread** —
  the freeze was complete by 123.6 s and no
  thread state moved for the remaining 780 s.
- Final-dump census (71 threads): 58 blocked —
  40 wineserver-socket reads
  (KUnixSocketObject::lockCond), 17 futex waits,
  1 timed poll (KThread::pollCond), the ordinary
  idle pattern — and **13 state=running, all in
  pid 10 (the main guest process, 44 threads),
  every one with wait=- (no wait condition),
  syscall -(0), frozen at guest instruction
  addresses**.
- Comparison: the pre-fix capture
  (test-results/fd12/consumer-hunt-4/) showed 69
  threads with 10 main-process threads frozen in
  the same running/no-condition shape.

**Verdict, in FD-77's own terms: the
constellation persisted.** On the shipped
fault-path-fixed build, through the public
route, a parked session still freezes as a
dozen-plus main-process threads running with no
wait condition — not as a single stranded
fetcher. FD-77 therefore stays open exactly as
filed; this deploy's tally is its first
public-route evidence: **5 presented / 2 stalled
across 7 consumer runs on this build** (5/1 in
the uninstrumented series, 0/1 in the armed
series). No claim is made beyond what the runs
and dumps show: the dumps read guest state only,
and which mechanism leaves those 13 threads
runnable-but-never-progressing cannot be
separated from this capture.
